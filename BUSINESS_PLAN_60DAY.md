## VALIDATION TRACK — proving the engine works

### Week 1-2: Data Foundation
The data audit is the gating item. Come out of it with a clear answer
to three questions: what data is actually available, how clean is it,
and what is the realistic insight surface area. Do not start the
validation clock until you know what you are working with. If the audit
reveals gaps, apply proxy metrics immediately rather than treating
missing data as a blocker — repeat-call rate, escalation rate, and
handle time variance are leading indicators if long-term churn data
is thin.

**Milestone:** Documented data inventory. Three specific insight
hypotheses written down before any analysis begins. Pre-stating
hypotheses is non-negotiable — it is what separates a real validation
from retrospective rationalization.

### Week 3-4: First Briefing Draft
Run the analysis. Produce a draft briefing as if it were going to a
real client. Format it for a Director-level reader — no raw data dumps,
no methodology footnotes. Recommendations with supporting evidence.
Aim for something that could be read in 15 minutes and acted on the
same day.

**Milestone:** One complete mock briefing exists. Production effort
timed. This tells you whether $2-5k/month is sustainable or whether
you are burning 40 hours per deliverable.

### Week 5-6: Blind Review
Send the briefing to two or three people who know contact center
operations but were not involved in producing it. Not for validation
of conclusions — for validation of whether the format, framing, and
recommendations land correctly. A smart insight buried in jargon
fails the client-readiness test.

**Milestone:** External feedback collected. At least one specific
recommendation from the briefing has been acted on or is being
considered in the real operation. First proof of business impact.

### Week 7-8: Repeatability Test
Produce a second briefing. Different time slice, different focus area.
The first briefing tells you the engine can run. The second tells you
it can run consistently without heroic effort. Document the production
process — what steps, what tools, what time — because this becomes
the operational model.

**Milestone:** Second briefing complete. Total time investment per
briefing quantified. Repeatable process documented, not just a
one-time result.

## PIPELINE WARMING TRACK — building the room where you will pitch

### Week 1-2: List and Map
Identify 15-20 Director/VP-level contacts in your network who run
mid-market contact center operations. Not prospects yet — people you
could have a real conversation with. LinkedIn, former colleagues,
industry connections. Prioritize people who will give honest reactions,
not just encouragement.

**Milestone:** List exists. Ranked by relationship warmth and relevance.

### Week 3-4: Curiosity Conversations (not pitches)
Reach out to 5-7 contacts per week with a specific ask: not "would
you buy this" but "I am building something and I need 20 minutes from
someone who actually runs one of these operations." Your 15 years is
the door-opener. You are not a vendor cold-calling — you are a peer
asking for a conversation.

In those conversations ask three things:
- What does your current platform tell you that you actually act on?
- What questions do you have at the end of every month that nothing
  answers well?
- If someone handed you a monthly briefing that answered those
  questions, what would make it worth paying for versus ignoring?

Do not pitch. Do not mention pricing. Just listen and take notes.

**Milestone:** 8-10 conversations completed. Pattern of what Directors
say they are missing identified. Anyone who asks "when can I see it?"
without being prompted is a warm lead — track them separately.

### Week 5-6: Tighten the Hypothesis
You now have validation data from the product side and language data
from the market side. Put them together. Where does what your briefing
actually surfaces overlap with what Directors said they are missing?
That overlap is your positioning. Where they do not overlap is either
a product gap or a messaging problem — determine which.

Draft a one-page overview. Not a deck, not a website — one page that
describes the problem, what you deliver, and what it costs. Designed
for a Director to forward to their VP without you in the room.

The forwarding test is the real test: if it cannot survive without
you explaining it, it is not ready.

**Milestone:** One-pager exists. Shown to 3 contacts with the question
"would you forward this to someone?" Their reaction tells you
everything.

### Week 7-8: Convert Warmth to Commitment
Go back to your warmest 3-5 contacts — the ones who asked follow-up
questions, who said "let me know when it is ready," who forwarded
the one-pager. Make the ask. Not a full sale — a pilot.

Framing: "I am running this for two clients at cost for 90 days in
exchange for honest feedback and a case study if it works. Interested?"

Pilot pricing at $500-800/month serves two purposes: it filters
serious interest from polite interest, and it generates first revenue
before month 6. Someone who will not pay $500 for a pilot will not
pay $3,000 for the real thing.

**Milestone:** At least 1 pilot conversation in progress. Ideally 1
signed. Even a "not now but follow up in August" is useful — log it
with a date and hold to it.

## How The Two Tracks Connect

By Week 6-7, validation results and pipeline conversations arrive at
the same moment. You walk into the pilot ask with:
- A real briefing you can show (sanitized)
- A production time you can defend
- Language borrowed directly from what prospects told you they needed

That is not a cold pitch — that is a demonstration that you already
understand their problem better than anyone selling them software does.

---

## The Constraint Worth Flagging

10 hours a week across both tracks is lean. Weeks 3-6 are the heaviest
overlap — producing the first briefing, running the blind review, and
having 5+ conversations per week simultaneously.

If something has to compress, compress the pipeline outreach cadence
before compressing the validation quality. A slightly slower pipeline
is recoverable. A validation with soft success criteria that you then
try to sell against is not.
